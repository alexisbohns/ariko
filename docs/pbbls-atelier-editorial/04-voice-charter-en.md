# Voice charter — writing Pebbles for Ariko

This is the brief for the **English** pages on Ariko. It is not the atelier voice
(`03-guide-de-style.md`, French, first person, apartés) and it is not the Paulopus
voice either. It sits between them, deliberately.

> **One sentence:** the rigour of a good post-mortem, delivered by someone who is
> genuinely funny and has nothing to prove.

Read `03-guide-de-style.md` for who Alexis is. Read a live Paulopus bean
(`ariko.app/bean/match-anatomy`) for the evidentiary standard. Then write like
neither, per below.

---

## 1. The two inheritances

**From Paulopus — keep all of it:**

- Every claim has a page underneath it. If it can't be sourced, it doesn't ship.
- Precise numbers, precise dates, precise names. "64 deliverables between 20 May
  and 30 July" beats "a busy summer".
- No announcement register. No "we're excited to". No "seamless", "leverage",
  "journey", "delightful", "robust".
- **Never overclaim.** "Shipped, then re-opened" is not the same as "stabilised".
  "Designed and never wired" is a real and respectable sentence. Use it.

**From the atelier — bring these, and only these:**

- First person singular, where it is honest. "I deleted it." Not "the decision was
  taken to remove".
- Short fragments as beats. One per section, maybe two. They are punctuation, not
  a style.
- Failure narrated plainly, without flinching and without wallowing.
- Jargon gets an image before it gets a definition.

## 1a. INTENTION BEFORE MECHANISM — the first rule

Both rejected pilots failed the same way underneath their other problems: they
were **technical, executional, operational, tactical — and had no vision in them.**
They said what was built and how it works. They never said what it is *for*.

A reader who finishes your piece should be able to answer:

- **What is this for?** What does it let a person do, or protect them from?
- **Why does it exist at all?** What would be worse without it?
- **Why this way?** What value or belief does the shape of the choice express?

Mechanism without intention is a manual. **The intention is the piece; the
mechanism is the evidence.**

### Where it goes

Near the top — usually the paragraph right after the situation, before any
implementation. Then again, briefly, at the turn: when a decision cost something,
say what it was protecting that made the cost worth paying.

Not a mission-statement paragraph. One or two sentences of plain purpose, in the
same register as everything else.

### The three levels

- **Plant** — the product's vision. Why Pebbles exists at all.
- **Pod** — what this territory is for. Why an economy; why a public face.
- **Bean** — why this specific thing exists, and what it protects or enables.

A bean's intention should connect upward. If you cannot see how it does, that is
worth flagging (below) rather than papering over.

### NEVER INVENT AN INTENTION

This is the hardest rule and the one that matters most. A plausible-sounding
purpose you reasoned out yourself is **worse than silence** — it puts a belief in
Alexis's mouth that he may not hold, in the one register where being wrong is not
a factual error but a misrepresentation.

If the sources do not give you the why, you have exactly two moves:

1. **Write nothing.** State the mechanism plainly and stop. A piece that explains
   what something does without claiming to know why is honest.
2. **Propose it inline, marked, for review:**

   ```
   [INTENTION? — I could not source the why here. Proposal: the market is
   community-supplied so the symbols in someone's journal come from other
   people rather than from a design team. Accept, rewrite, or cut at review.]
   ```

   One proposal, clearly marked, clearly deletable. Never more than one sentence
   of proposal, and never woven into the prose so it reads as fact.

Use `[TO VERIFY: …]` for facts you could not confirm and `[INTENTION? — …]` for
purpose you could not source. They are different problems and get different marks.

### `brouillons/` IS NOT A SOURCE OF INTENTION

The drafts in `docs/pbbls-atelier-editorial/brouillons/` (B1–B6) were **written by
agents in May 2026**, not by Alexis. `_notes-relecture.md` calls them
échafaudages — scaffolds — and lists the things only he can decide. He has never
signed off on their reasoning.

They are excellent for **facts, dates, quotations and structure**. They are
**not evidence of what Alexis believes**, and an explanation found only there is
an earlier agent's inference, not a source.

This has already gone wrong once: a May draft's reasoning about why *bounce* beat
*skimmer* was picked up months later and written as his, purely because it was
written down. **A rationale does not become sourced by being in a file.** If the
only place a why appears is a `brouillons/` draft, mark it — and say in the marker
that the draft is agent-written, so he knows what he is approving.

The same caution applies to any `_digests/*.md`: those are agent summaries of
primary sources. Facts, yes. Purpose, only where they quote the primary source
directly.

**Sourced intentions you may state as fact** (author, 2026-09-02, recorded in the
case-study spec §4): karma, badges and the market exist to reward and sustain the
habit, not to imprison; karma is earned by recording, which requires having lived
something, and spent on glyphs that decorate memories made outside the app;
time-in-app is not the metric; "no feed to scroll" is kept absolutely — no feed,
no directory, no search, no follower graph, connections by invite or QR only.

Anything beyond that list, you mark.

## 1b. What a draft is for — do not force the jokes

**Alexis adds the humour himself, in his own edit pass, by reformulating.** That
is where the piece becomes his rather than merely accurate.

Your job is the scaffold he reformulates: the true story, in the right order, in
warm plain prose, with nothing to unpick. A draft that is dry and correct is
useful to him. A draft padded with manufactured wit is *worse than useless*,
because he has to strip it before he can start.

So: if a funny line arrives naturally and is also exactly true, keep it. **Never
reach for one.** An earnest sentence beats a joke that had to be assembled, every
time. Leave the room; do not furnish it.

## 2. The humour

Dry, understated, load-bearing. The test is strict:

> **A joke earns its place only if it is also the most accurate sentence available.**

If the funny version is 5% less true, cut it. Humour here is compression, not
decoration — it is what lets you say something slightly damning about your own
work without it becoming a confession.

**Hard limits.** These are not suggestions:

- **One bracketed aside `[...]` per piece. Maximum.** The atelier runs three per
  section in French; in English at that density it reads as a tic, not a voice.
- **No meme name-dropping.** No Huh Cat, no "rideau, THE END", no emoji strings.
  The French drafts lean on these; they do not survive the crossing.
- **No more than one em-dash punchline per section.**
- Zero self-congratulation. Zero false modesty either — false modesty is just
  self-congratulation with extra steps.

## 3. Calibration

The same fact, three ways. Only the third ships.

> ❌ **Corporate:** "A strategic simplification of the recording experience reduced
> friction and improved time-to-value."
>
> ❌ **Atelier-in-English:** "So there I was, Sunday, coffee gone cold, staring at a
> pebble engine that had eaten my entire weekend. [Yes. Judge away.]"
>
> ✅ **Target:** "I spent a Sunday building a pebble engine that was too clever to
> finish, then deleted it. The mistake wasn't the engine — it was slicing the work
> back-to-front instead of in thin full-stack passes. The second engine took a day
> and was worse in every way that didn't matter."

Two more, drawn from real material:

> ✅ "There is no `CHECK (balance >= 0)` on the karma ledger. A column constraint
> would have rolled back a legitimate pebble deletion, so debt is allowed by
> design and the guard lives one level up, in a row-locked balance check inside
> `spend_karma`. You can go negative. You just can't spend your way there."

> ✅ "`refund_karma` shipped granted to `authenticated`. Six minutes later a
> migration titled 'Security fix' took it away, because any user could have minted
> themselves a million karma. The spec had said so. The migration hadn't read it."

Note what those do: the humour is entirely in the accuracy. Nothing is added.

## 4. Person and tense

- **First person singular** for decisions, mistakes, and taste. "I chose", "I was
  wrong about".
- **Third person, present tense** for how the system behaves. "The ledger allows
  debt." Not "I made the ledger allow debt."
- **Past tense** for what happened, with dates.
- "We" only when other humans were genuinely involved — and name them.

## 4b. STORY, NOT LIST — read this before you write a word

The first pilot failed here, completely, and it is the easiest failure to repeat.

**A piece is a story with a question at the front, not a thesis with evidence
underneath.** If your draft could be reordered without loss, it is a list. If a
section could be deleted without the reader noticing, it is a list. If the reader
cannot say what the *problem* was after the first paragraph, it is a list.

Look at how Paulopus opens: the tournament dates, what the thing is, the joke
("an octopus predicting football is absurd on its face"), and then the real
question underneath it. Context, then self-aware premise, then a question the
reader now wants answered. It never opens with a score.

### The five beats every piece has

1. **A concrete situation**, in the second person or a scene. Someone is trying to
   do something ordinary.
2. **The snag.** Why the ordinary thing is not ordinary here.
3. **What was tried**, including what failed. Real attempts, in order.
4. **The turn.** The decision, and what it cost — because it always cost
   something.
5. **Where it stands**, dated, with the open question named.

### Numbers

**Three to five numbers in a whole piece. Not more.** Each one must change what
the reader believes. A census of model names, a distribution table, a count of
files — these interest the person who queried them and nobody else. If a number
is not load-bearing, cut it. Prefer "four in five" to "285 of 365".

**Every absolute number carries a date** (`as of 2 September 2026`) because the
repository moves. Ratios age better than counts; prefer them.

### Sourcing without choking the prose

Source comments are for facts a reader might doubt: a decision, a date, a
quotation, a design claim. **Not every sentence.** If a paragraph has more than
two, the paragraph is inventory, not narrative.

### The opening

Never open with a statistic. Never open with a credential. Never open with what
you built. Open with the **problem**, and preferably with somebody hitting it.

The rejected pilot opened "364 commits on `main`. Every one authored by me." That
is a flex wearing a fact, and it tells the reader nothing they wanted to know.

## 5. Structure of a bean piece

1. **Opening**: the problem, stated concretely, in under 80 words. No throat-clearing,
   no "in this piece I will".
2. **Three to six `##` sections**, each named for the thing it explains — not
   "Background", not "Challenges". Name the actual object: "One ledger, and no
   floor at zero".
3. **The reversal or the cost.** Every piece has one. What broke, what got dropped,
   what was decided twice. If a piece has no reversal, it is probably not finished
   being researched.
4. **Where it stands now**, dated, with the open question named if there is one.

Target 1,800–3,000 words. Longer needs a reason.

## 5b. Trust the neighbours

There are 42 beans, and every one of them exists — six written, the rest private
stubs (`payloads/_SLUGS.md`). **Your piece is one of them and does not have to
explain the product.** When a subject belongs to another bean, name it in one
clause, link it, and move on.

Mid-sentence, the link is `:entity[Label]{ref=bean:slug}` — **single colon, with
a label**. The double-colon `::entity{ref=…}` form is a *block* directive and is
inert inside a paragraph: it renders as literal text. Every ref in the first wave
was written the wrong way for this reason. Use `::entity{ref=…}` only on a line
of its own, where you want a card.

This is where list-creep comes back in disguise. The D8 pilot drifted from "the
lock" into purchase transactions, unique constraints, default prices, delisting,
reattribution and account deletion — six subjects, five of which have their own
bean. Each addition looked like context and the sum was a tour.

The test: **if a paragraph would sit just as naturally in a different bean, it
belongs there.** Cut it, and trust the reader to follow a link.

A piece that stays on one subject and ends early is the goal, not a shortfall.

## 6. Sourcing rules

- Every date, number, and quoted rationale traces to the pbbls repo: a decision-log
  entry (with its date and issue number), a spec file, a journal event, or a
  migration. Cite it in the draft as a trailing comment the editor can strip:
  `<!-- src: docs/decisions/log.md 2026-06-30 #496 -->`
- **Never invent.** If a fact is missing, write `[TO VERIFY: what you need]` inline.
  A draft with ten honest gaps is worth more than one with a single invented detail.
- The **April–May 2026 corpus is not current.** Anything drawn from
  `00-chronologie.md`, `_digests/apple-journal.md`, `_digests/gemini.md` or the
  `brouillons/` must be checked against the `_digests/*-current.md` files before it
  is stated as present-tense fact.
- Where the product changed its mind, **say so and date both positions.** That is
  the most valuable thing these pages contain.

## 7. Proportion — the most important rule here

Most material in this corpus can be told two ways: as evidence of **competence**
or as a **confession**. The facts almost always support the first. Choosing the
second is not honesty, it is drama, and it misleads the reader about what
actually happened.

Before framing anything as a failure, near-miss, or narrow escape, check what the
record actually says. A problem found by a deliberate audit is proof the audit
works. A design revisited after new evidence is engineering, not error. A feature
cut for scope is a decision, not a defeat.

**Self-deprecation is not candour.** Alexis's voice is genuinely self-deprecating
and that stays — but it is the humour of someone secure in their competence, never
the apology of someone expecting to be caught. The register is *"that was the
wrong cut, and here is the right one"*, never *"look how badly I messed this up."*

### Performed humility is the same failure as the flex

Watch for the second trap, which is subtler and which the pilots kept falling
into: **dramatizing your own modesty.** These are all the same move as bragging,
just inverted —

> ❌ "I'd like to tell you this came from a clean principle. It didn't."
> ❌ "Honestly, it started as a mess."
> ❌ "It took me embarrassingly long to see."
> ❌ "I'm not sure I'd have got there without…"

Each one asks the reader to admire the admission. State the reasons and stop:

> ✅ "Two things motivated the read-only lock. The first was delete: … The second
> is what a buyer is actually paying for — not a cat, but those strokes, in that
> order, wobble included."

**Do not narrate your own reasoning process at all** unless the sequence is itself
the point and the record supports it. Say what was decided and why. The humility
lives in the absence of decoration, not in a confession bolted to the front.

Neither perform mastery nor perform modesty. Just say the thing.

Concretely, never reach for: `breach`, `leak`, `incident`, `alarming`, `scary`,
`P0`, `disaster`, `embarrassing`, `luckily`, `thankfully`, `narrowly avoided`,
`could have been much worse`. If a sentence's energy comes from how bad something
might have been rather than from what was built, rewrite it.

See §8 of the case-study spec for how this applies to the security and quality
material specifically — that frame is decided and binding.

## 7b. The ethical position (settled — carry it, don't relitigate)

Pebbles argued in April 2026 against streaks, punishment and dark patterns. By
July 2026 it had a currency, debt, a marketplace and permanent badges. The
author's position, recorded 2026-09-02:

> Badges, currency and the market exist to **reward** and to sustain the habit,
> not to imprison. Karma is earned by recording — which requires having lived
> something — and spent on glyphs that decorate memories made outside the app.
> The aim is to get someone out into their life to collect something worth
> keeping, not to hold them on a screen. **Time-in-app is not the metric.**

The April onboarding promise had two clauses: *"No streak to protect, no feed to
scroll."* The second is kept absolutely, and enforced by decree written before any
code: no feed, no directory, no search, no follower graph, connections by invite
or QR only. The first is *complicated* — bounce existed since April, but became
visible on a public profile in July.

**The position, recorded 2026-09-03 and statable as fact:** the mechanic never
broke the promise. Bounce is a rank on a rolling window that *erodes* — not a
chain that breaks and punishes. July changed **who can see it**, not what it does
to you, and April's promise was about punishment.

Still say plainly that the streak became social in July and that no decision
entry records the change. The position is that visibility is not punishment, not
that nothing happened. Do not overstate it into "the promise was never touched",
and do not flatten it into a betrayal.

**Why the market is community-supplied, recorded 2026-09-03:** so you can share
a mark you carved and be rewarded for sharing it, and so the symbols on someone's
memory were drawn by another person who records rather than by a design team.

⚠️ **With one factual caution: most community glyphs live today were drawn by
Alexis.** Never imply a large third-party creator base. That is the design's
intent, not yet its population.

**Act I is trimmed (2026-09-03).** Anything pre-March-2026 that traces only to a
digest or a `brouillons/` draft is **cut**, not marked — the primaries are in no
repo. Keep only what the pbbls repo, decision log, specs, migrations, changelog
or Arkaik journal carry on their own. See spec §4.2.

## 8. Bilingual

Every piece is authored in **both** English and French. The French is an
**adaptation, never a translation** — it may use the atelier's warmth more freely
(`03-guide-de-style.md`), but it carries the same facts, the same dates, the same
refusal to overclaim, and the same structure section-for-section.

The FR half may run the aparté gesture at atelier density. The EN half may not.
That asymmetry is intentional: the gesture is native to one language and imported
into the other.

## 9. Kill list

Never appears in an Ariko Pebbles page:

`seamless` · `leverage` · `robust` · `delightful` · `journey` (unless literal) ·
`game-changer` · `unlock` (metaphorical) · `deep dive` · `at the end of the day` ·
`it's worth noting that` · `arguably` · `in today's fast-paced` · any sentence
beginning "As a designer," · any paragraph that could open a LinkedIn post ·
emoji · exclamation marks (one per page, if truly earned) · rhetorical questions
used as section openers.
